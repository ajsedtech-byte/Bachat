What belongs on the blog

The blog should not be only announcements. It should explain common marketplace workflows in plain language: why detailed product requests matter, how seller quotes should be compared, how delivery updates work, what information is needed for refund review, and how sellers can reduce avoidable cancellations.

Because Bachat connects multiple roles, every useful article should help a real person take the next step with more confidence. A good post is specific, grounded in actual marketplace behavior, and clear about who it is written for.

Key editorial areas

  • Shopper educationGuides for writing better requests, comparing quotes, choosing sellers, tracking orders, and contacting support with the right details.
  • Seller playbooksPractical notes on onboarding, quote quality, catalog hygiene, pricing clarity, delivery readiness, customer trust, and order follow-up.
  • Delivery and fulfillmentExplanations of delivery availability, address quality, partner readiness, seller-managed fulfillment, and updates that reduce failed coordination.
  • Platform updatesRelease notes that explain dashboard improvements, support changes, payment flows, KYC updates, notification changes, and city-level launches.

Editorial structure

The best Bachat content should be written with a clear purpose. Before publishing, each article should answer: who is this for, what confusion does it reduce, what action should the reader take, and which part of the marketplace does it improve?

Guides should be step-by-step

Educational posts should break workflows into simple steps. For example, a shopper article can explain how to describe a product request, what details to include, how to compare seller responses, and when to contact support.

Seller content should be operational

Seller articles should focus on practical execution: how to quote clearly, how to avoid missed expectations, how to keep catalog information useful, and how to respond when an order needs readiness or delivery coordination.

Updates should explain impact

Platform updates should explain the benefit behind the change. A dashboard update is useful when sellers know how it saves time. A support update matters when shoppers understand what information helps the team resolve a case faster.

Examples should feel local

Bachat content should reflect real city-level buying behavior: nearby shops, urgent needs, delivery constraints, price comparison, payment confidence, seller availability, and the practical trust that local commerce depends on.

Publishing principles

Be specific: write about real workflows, not generic marketplace advice. Be useful: help the reader complete a task or avoid a common mistake. Be transparent: explain limitations, support requirements, and what users should expect next.
